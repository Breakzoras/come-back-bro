# Research A: early romantic love, limerence, and what regulates them

Scope: evidence base for the "come-back-bro" (intoxicated mode) skill, axis = psychology of early romantic love, limerence, rumination, uncertainty, attachment, and referral criteria.
Date of research: 2026-11-02. Every source below was opened during this session. PubMed records were read through the PubMed API (abstract level) and are cited as pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMID plus DOI. Where only a secondary page (news, digest) was reachable, that is stated.

Strength scale: strong = meta-analysis or several converging controlled studies; moderate = one or a few controlled studies with reasonable samples; weak = small single study, qualitative work, case study, conference paper, or unreplicated/failed replication.

## 0. Population caveat (read first)

- The love-regulation experiments (Langeslag) used people after a breakup or people in established relationships, measured within one lab session. Effects are short-term and in-the-moment.
- Limerence research studies prolonged, mostly unreciprocated, impairing states recruited from limerence support forums.
- The skill's user is in the first weeks of early, possibly mutual dating. So: limerence findings work as red-flag criteria; the skill should never apply the label "limerence" by default. Early infatuation with frequent, enjoyable thoughts is the normal case.

## 1. Verified findings

### A. Love regulation (Langeslag and colleagues)

A1. Love feelings can be deliberately turned up or down with reappraisal, in the short term.
- Langeslag & van Strien (2016), PLoS ONE. Study 1 N=32 (questionnaire, mostly women, 84% in a relationship). Study 2 N=40 (20 in a relationship, 20 after a breakup; 36 with usable EEG). Down-regulation (thinking about negative aspects of the partner, relationship or future, e.g. "She is so lazy", "We won't stay together forever") decreased infatuation and attachment; up-regulation (positive aspects, e.g. "He is so funny") increased attachment. Down-regulation effects were larger (about 0.5 to 1.0 points on a 1-5 scale) than up-regulation (about 0.1 to 0.3). Down-regulation also increased unpleasant feelings. Authors' limits: small exploratory samples, no correction for multiple tests, demand characteristics, LPP is indirect, short-term only.
- Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27529751/ , https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161087 , full text read at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4987042/
- Strength: weak to moderate (small N, single lab, replicated in direction by A2).

A2. After a breakup: negative reappraisal lowers love but worsens mood; distraction improves mood but leaves love unchanged; "love reappraisal" changed neither.
- Langeslag & Sanchez (2018, online 2017), J Exp Psychol Gen 147:720-733. Participants upset about a breakup viewed photos of the ex after each strategy. Negative reappraisal decreased love feelings and made participants feel more unpleasant. Love reappraisal (e.g. "It's okay to love someone I'm no longer with") did not change love or mood. Distraction (thinking about neutral things such as favourite food) did not change love but made participants feel more pleasant. All three strategies reduced the LPP (motivated attention) to the ex's photo.
- N=24 (20 women, 4 men): this number comes from secondary pages only (BPS Research Digest and UMSL news), the paper itself was not reachable.
- Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28857575/ , https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000360 , https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/researchers-have-compared-different-cognitive-strategies-falling-out-love , https://blogs.umsl.edu/news/2018/06/01/sandra-langeslag-breakups (Langeslag quoted: "Love regulation doesn't work like an on/off switch"; lasting change probably needs regular regulation).
- Strength: weak to moderate (small N, gender imbalance, immediate effects only).

A3. Love regulation and emotion regulation are distinct; looking at the beloved's pictures increases love.
- Langeslag (2024), Behav Sci 14:383, review "Refuting six misconceptions about romantic love". Key points read in full text: distraction after a breakup decreased negative affect but left love intensity unchanged, negative reappraisal decreased love but increased negative affect, so "love regulation targets love feelings ... whereas emotion regulation targets emotions". Also: "looking at pictures of the beloved increases infatuation and attachment", positive reappraisal increases attachment, sexual imagery increases desire and infatuation. Love has negative effects too: infatuation is stressful, people in love may be distracted from work, and love is associated with stalking. Serotonin-low "love is like OCD" is a hypothesis, one study found the opposite direction in women.
- Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38785874/ , https://doi.org/10.3390/bs14050383 (full text via PMC11117554 through the PubMed API)
- Strength: review by the main researcher (moderate as a summary; underlying studies are small).

A4. People in love think about the beloved most of the day and mostly enjoy it; more thinking goes with more distraction and some task cost.
- Langeslag & Philippi (Cognition & Emotion, online 2024, print 2025, 39:920-927). Participants in love reported thinking about the beloved 67% of the time in general and up to 42% during a task. More such thinking related to more self-reported distraction, worse response withholding and slower responses. "Participants overwhelmingly enjoyed thinking about their beloved."
- N not stated in the abstract; full text blocked (403).
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39431980/ , https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2024.2417840
- Strength: moderate for the descriptive finding, weak for causality (correlational).

A5. Related: the beloved captures attention automatically (EPN, LPP) and obsessive thinking tends to correlate with poorer task performance.
- Langeslag & van Strien (2018) Neuropsychologia, two ERP studies of people recently in love: beloved faces got preferential processing even when task-irrelevant; task performance did not differ by face type, but self-reported obsessive thinking "tended to correlate negatively" with performance. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28927655/ , https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2017.09.015
- Langeslag (2022) review of EEG/ERP studies: beloved stimuli reliably elicit an enhanced LPP and EPN across cultures. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35624939/ , https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci12050551
- Strength: moderate (consistent ERP finding; performance link weak).

A6. People believe they cannot down-regulate infatuation; habitual reappraisers believe they can.
- Surti & Langeslag (2019) PLoS ONE, N=286 online: participants thought they could up- but not down-regulate love, and infatuation least of all; habitual reappraisal use predicted higher perceived ability. Authors frame this as a target for psychoeducation.
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31083671/ , https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0216523
- Strength: moderate (perceptions only, self-report).

A7. 2020+ love-regulation work found: Langeslag & Davis (2022) J Sex Med, N=25, up-regulation of desire for a long-term partner (sexual imagery raised desire and infatuation; positive reappraisal raised attachment). Not directly relevant to early dating. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35304850/ , https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsxm.2022.02.017 . Weak (small N). No 2020+ study of love down-regulation in early, mutual dating was found.

### B. Limerence

B1. Concept. Tennov (1979) coined limerence: an obsessive desire for emotional reciprocation from one person; Tennov and Wakin & Vo distinguish it from ordinary early infatuation by the degree of preoccupation and day-to-day impairment. Read as summarized in Marshall et al. 2025 (Tennov's book itself not opened).
- Source: https://eprints.chi.ac.uk/8379/1/marshall-et-al-2025-development-and-validation-of-the-limerence-questionnaire-%28lq-11%29.pdf (text extracted and read)
- Note: the claim that "greater uncertainty about the other's feelings means greater desire and rumination" is cited in that paper to Wyant (2021), a single case study. Strength of that specific claim: weak.

B2. Wakin & Vo (2008), conference paper (Inter-Disciplinary.Net, "Challenging Intimate Boundaries"): limerence is an involuntary interpersonal state with intrusive, obsessive and compulsive thoughts, feelings and behaviours contingent on perceived reciprocation; resembles normal love but is "necessarily negative, problematic and impairing"; three components (initiating, driving, resultant forces); compared to OCD and substance dependence.
- Source: https://digitalcommons.sacredheart.edu/psych_fac/131
- Strength: weak (theoretical model presented at a conference, without journal peer review or data).

B3. Willmott & Bentley (2015), The Qualitative Report 20(1):20-38. IPA with N=6. "Limerence trajectory": ruminative thinking, free-floating anxiety, temporary fixation, depression, disintegration of self; involvement of both a real and an idealized limerent object, possibly linked to attachment anxiety; overlaps with OCD, addiction, separation anxiety, maladaptive fantasy.
- Source: https://nsuworks.nova.edu/tqr/vol20/iss1/2/ , https://doi.org/10.46743/2160-3715/2015.1420
- Strength: weak (qualitative, N=6).

B4. First validated limerence scale: LQ-11 (Marshall, Waldeck, Pancani, Churchill, Tyndall, 2025), Psychological Reports. Study 1 N=269 (EFA), Study 2 N=401 (CFA). Two factors: "Intense Need for Attachment" (alpha .92; e.g. "I have/had a strong longing for reciprocation from them") and "Neglect to Self and Others" (alpha .89; e.g. "relationships and responsibilities that are neglected due to excessive thinking about them"). Correlates: self-esteem, psychological inflexibility (small), need to belong, imaginative fantasy; anxious attachment correlated with the attachment factor (not with neglect); avoidant attachment not related. Recruited via limerence support forums (e.g. limerence.net); WEIRD samples; self-report only; no test-retest; stalking and distress outcomes not measured.
- Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41178753/ , https://doi.org/10.1177/00332941251394980 , PDF read: https://eprints.chi.ac.uk/8379/1/marshall-et-al-2025-development-and-validation-of-the-limerence-questionnaire-%28lq-11%29.pdf
- Strength: moderate for the psychometrics, preliminary for clinical use.

B5. Largest limerence study to date: Evans, Panton, Strawson, Floyd, Kellett, Poerio (2026), Acta Psychologica 267:107043. Study 1 N=1647 survey: onset around age 18, about 5 episodes across adulthood, episodes with about 2-year fixation; elevated adverse childhood experiences, insecure attachment, obsessive-compulsive traits; 42% met caseness for several conditions at once (anxiety, depression, dissociation, maladaptive daydreaming). Study 2 N=51 experience sampling over 7 days: thoughts about the limerent object were about 50% of waking thought, intrusive and immersive, linked to negative affect; "intrusive-distracting fantasy" dominated, especially during sadness and loneliness. Authors call for diagnostic criteria and treatment trials.
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42127691/ , https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.107043 (full text blocked, abstract only)
- Strength: moderate (large N, but self-selected impairing-limerence sample).

B6. Contrast with A4: in ordinary early love, thought frequency is similarly high (67%) but enjoyed (Langeslag & Philippi). In impairing limerence the thoughts are intrusive and tied to sadness and loneliness (Evans 2026). The distinguishing marker is the quality and cost of the thoughts, and frequency alone does not separate the two.

B7. Treatment evidence for limerence: one case study (Wyant 2021, J Patient Exp, N=1) using CBT with exposure and response prevention; compulsive rituals decreased at 9-month follow-up; the author's screening tool is unvalidated.
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34869848/ , https://doi.org/10.1177/23743735211060812
- Strength: weak.

B8. Related clinical category: relationship OCD (ROCD). Doron et al. (2016), Front Psychiatry: 22 ROCD clients, 22 OCD clients, 28 controls; ROCD primary-symptom severity comparable to OCD, more depression than controls. Concerns obsessions about an existing relationship or partner, which is a different focus from early-dating limerence.
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27148087/ , https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2016.00058
- Strength: weak to moderate (small clinical samples).

B9. Early love and sleep. Brand et al. (2007), J Adolesc Health, N=107 adolescents (60 recently and intensely in love): higher hypomania scores, more positive mood, fewer hours of sleep yet better subjective sleep quality, less daytime sleepiness and better concentration than controls.
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17577536/ , https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jadohealth.2007.01.012
- Strength: weak to moderate (adolescents, cross-sectional, 7-night self-report log). Implication: shorter sleep in early love is common; on its own it signals little about harm.

### C. Rumination, intrusive thoughts, suppression, writing

C1. Thought suppression rebounds. Wang, Hagger & Chatzisarantis (2020) meta-analysis of 31 studies in Wegner's paradigm: rebound after suppression appeared regardless of cognitive load; immediate enhancement appeared only under cognitive load. Earlier meta-analysis (Abramowitz, Tolin & Street 2001): small to moderate rebound effect.
- Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32286932/ , https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691619898795 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11434226/ , https://doi.org/10.1016/s0272-7358(00)00057-x
- Strength: strong for existence of rebound; effect size small to moderate.

C2. Suppressing thoughts of a still-desired person. Wegner & Gold (1995), JPSP, 2 experiments: suppressing a no-longer-desired old flame produced a rebound of thoughts; suppressing a still-desired relationship produced no thought rebound but increased skin conductance (physiological arousal).
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7776182/ , https://doi.org/10.1037//0022-3514.68.5.782
- Strength: moderate (old study, specific paradigm).

C3. Anxious attachment and suppression. Mikulincer, Dolev & Shaver (2004), JPSP 87:940-956. Study 1 N=120, Study 2 N=200 Israeli undergraduates, asked to suppress thoughts of a breakup. Attachment anxiety was associated with more frequent breakup thoughts even under suppression instructions, and with higher accessibility of separation thoughts and negative self-representations.
- Source (full text read): https://adultattachment.faculty.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/66/2015/09/Mikulincer_2004_Attachment-related-strategies-during-thought-suppression.pdf , https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.87.6.940
- Strength: moderate.

C4. Worry postponement (scheduled worry time): mixed evidence.
- McCarrick et al. (2025) Psychol Health, 4-arm online RCT, N=186, 14 days: postponement plus "if-then" planning reduced daily worry duration by about 15 minutes relative to plain postponement; no arm improved sleep outcomes. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41347618/ , https://doi.org/10.1080/08870446.2025.2590072
- Versluis, Verkuil, Brosschot et al. (2015 online) Br J Health Psychol, online RCT, 361 completers of 996: postponement to a 30-minute evening slot did not reduce worry frequency, duration or complaints vs registration only. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26511764/ , https://doi.org/10.1111/bjhp.12170
- 2019 student sample N=114: no effect on worry. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30740991/ , https://doi.org/10.1080/08870446.2018.1562066
- 2024 waitlist RCT in GAD (n=47) and hypochondriasis (n=35): lower worry in the treated GAD group only. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39119056/ , https://doi.org/10.32872/cpe.12741
- Strength: weak to moderate; the specific if-then plan version has the best single result.

C5. Concrete, specific thinking beats abstract "why" thinking during rumination. Watkins (2009) review of experimental work; Watkins et al. (2012) Psychol Med Phase II RCT, N=121 with major depression: concreteness training added to usual care improved depression and reduced rumination; it did not differ from relaxation training on symptoms but beat it on rumination.
- Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19697180/ , https://doi.org/10.1080/16506070902980695 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22085757/ , https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291711002480
- Strength: moderate (clinical depression sample; generalization to dating anxiety is an extrapolation).

C6. Treatments for rumination and worry in general: systematic review of 19 studies (2002-2012) found mindfulness-based and CBT interventions may reduce both. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24036088/ , https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2013.08.004 . Students: meta-analysis of 51 RCTs, mindfulness improved rumination (small to moderate vs passive controls), with no benefit for worry or thought suppression; risk of bias generally high. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31743957/ , https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12188 . Strength: moderate.

C7. Activity: exercise meta-analysis in depressed adults (5 RCTs, 260 participants for rumination): moderate-intensity aerobic exercise reduced rumination (SMD -0.59); authors call findings preliminary. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39923828/ , https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychsport.2025.102824 . Strength: weak to moderate. Behavioral activation specifically for rumination: no primary source was verified in this session (not verified).

C8. Expressive writing: small average effect and can backfire.
- Frattaroli (2006) Psychol Bull meta-analysis of 146 randomized studies: average r = .075 (small). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17073523/ , https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.823
- Sbarra et al. (2013) Clin Psychol Sci, N=90 recently separated adults: participants actively searching for meaning about the separation had worse emotional outcomes up to 9 months later in either expressive-writing condition than in control writing. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25606351/ , https://doi.org/10.1177/2167702612469801
- Strength: strong that average effect is small; moderate that it can harm ruminators.

### D. Uncertainty and attraction

D1. Whitchurch, Wilson & Gilbert (2011), Psychol Sci 22:172-175. N=47 female undergraduates viewed fictitious Facebook profiles of men said to like them a lot, an average amount, or uncertain. Uncertain condition: most attraction (t(44)=2.07, p=.04 vs liked-best) and more thoughts about the men (p=.05 vs liked-best). Mediation through thinking was only marginal (p=.10) and the bootstrapped indirect-effect interval included zero. Authors' own limit: unclear whether this holds once people meet and begin a relationship; only women.
- Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21169522/ , https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797610393745 , full PDF read: https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/Whitchurch%20et%20al%20(2011).pdf
- Replication attempt: master's thesis by Annie T. McCord (Western Carolina University, repository dated 2017, submitted 2016), N=63 men and women, Tinder-like task with pupil dilation: "no significant effect of uncertainty on attraction, physiologically or psychologically" (unreviewed). Record opened: https://wcu.hykuup.com/concern/scholarly_works/1bca2d5b-c605-457d-b557-d33aabfd7a27 (the CORE PDF returned 403).
- Strength: weak.

D2. Playing hard to get. Birnbaum & Reis (2020), J Soc Pers Relat, 3 studies: a more selective or effortful-to-win partner was rated more desirable; authors caution it backfires if it looks like disinterest and seems to work only when the pursuer believes effort will succeed. Read via University of Rochester news only; N not available. https://www.rochester.edu/newscenter/why-playing-hard-to-get-may-work-435602/ . Strength: weak (secondary source).

D3. Responsiveness and questions build attraction.
- Birnbaum & Reis (2012) PSPB, 3 studies with strangers: perceiving a partner as responsive increased sexual interest, mainly among less avoidant people. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22517110/ , https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167212441028
- Huang et al. (2017) JPSP, 3 studies of live conversations incl. speed dating: more questions, especially follow-up questions, increased liking and second-date agreement; mediated by perceived responsiveness. 2025 correction after an independent audit: minor reporting errors, all conclusions upheld. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28447835/ , https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000097 ; correction https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40111841/
- Collins & Miller (1994) Psychol Bull meta-analysis: people who disclose more intimately are liked more; people disclose more to those they like; people like others after disclosing to them; moderated by paradigm, disclosure type, gender. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7809308/ , https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.3.457
- Strength: moderate to strong for responsiveness and questions; moderate for disclosure.

D4. Implication for a grounding coach: uncertainty plausibly increases how often the mind returns to the person, which the user experiences as intensity. That is a reason to explain the feeling. Evidence for using uncertainty as a dating tactic is weak, and responsiveness has better support.

### E. Attachment in early dating

E1. Hyperactivating strategies (attachment anxiety). Mikulincer & Shaver (2005), Personal Relationships 12:149-168 (full text read): energetic, insistent attempts to get proximity and love; "clinging and controlling responses", overdependence, vigilance to threat cues and a lowered threshold for detecting signs of unavailability, intensified negative emotion and rumination keeping concerns active in working memory. Anxious people may react to a partner's positive behaviour with ambivalence and fear.
- Source: https://adultattachment.faculty.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/66/2015/09/Mikulincer_2005_Attachment-theory-and-emotion-in-close-relationships.pdf
- Strength: strong as a theoretical synthesis of a large literature. Note: the popular term "protest behaviors" did not appear in any primary source opened here.

E2. Excessive reassurance seeking. Shaver, Schachner & Mikulincer (2005) PSPB: 72 couples, then 61 couples with 14-day diaries; reassurance seeking related to depression only through attachment anxiety; partners noticed it. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15657450/ , https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167204271709 . Strength: moderate.

E3. Security priming (reminders of being supported) improves affect. Gillath et al. (2022) PSPR meta-analysis: 120 studies, N=18,949, overall g=.51, largest for affect (.62); effects of subliminal priming on behaviour larger in more anxious and avoidant people; mixed evidence for publication bias; corrigendum published 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35209765/ , https://doi.org/10.1177/10888683211054592 ; corrigendum https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39275968/ . Strength: moderate (lab priming, short-lived, bias concerns).

E4. Social media surveillance and longing. Marshall (2012), Cyberpsychol Behav Soc Netw, N=464: Facebook surveillance of an ex-partner was associated with more distress, negative feelings, sexual desire, longing and less personal growth (cross-sectional). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22946958/ , https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2012.0125 . Combined with A3 ("looking at pictures of the beloved increases infatuation"), this supports the skill's anti-digging rule. Strength: moderate (correlational; breakup samples).

### F. When to refer to a professional

F1. Obsessive relational intrusion (ORI). Cupach & Spitzberg (2000) Violence Vict: ORI = "repeated and unwanted pursuit and invasion of one's sense of physical or symbolic privacy by another person ... who desires and/or presumes an intimate relationship". 63 behaviours, experienced by 3-78% of respondents; four factors: pursuit, violation, threat, hyper-intimacy; virtually all perceived as annoying, women rated them more threatening. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11288935/ . The ORI scale is the standard instrument for stalking perpetration and victimization in later studies (e.g. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22203630/ , where insecure attachment predicted stalking perpetration in women, N=934, small R2). Strength: moderate. Prevalence figures from Spitzberg & Cupach 2007 could not be verified (publisher and OJP pages did not load).

F2. Love and harm. Langeslag (2024) lists stalking, suicide behaviour, delusional disorders among negative effects of love (A3). Earp et al. (2017) argue that treatment decisions about love-related problems should hinge on harm and well-being, independent of disease labels. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28381923/ , https://doi.org/10.1353/ppp.2017.0011 . Strength: conceptual.

F3. Markers supported by the limerence literature: neglect of responsibilities and relationships (LQ-11 Neglect factor), intrusive thoughts linked to sadness and loneliness, prolonged duration (about 2 years) and recurring episodes (about 5), co-occurring anxiety, depression or dissociation (42% multimorbidity) (B4, B5). No validated time cutoff for "how many days of preoccupation" exists for limerence. NIMH describes OCD symptoms as "time-consuming" and causing "significant distress or interfere with daily life"; crisis guidance: call or text 988 (US), 911 in immediate danger. https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/obsessive-compulsive-disorder-ocd , https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/suicide-prevention . A DSM time criterion was not verified in this session.

### G. Other checks on SKILL.md claims

G1. Physiological sigh: Balban et al. (2023) Cell Rep Med, remote RCT: 5 minutes daily of cyclic sighing for one month improved mood and lowered respiratory rate more than mindfulness meditation. A single sigh before a date was not tested. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/ , https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895 . N not stated in abstract. Strength: moderate for daily practice; single-use is an extrapolation.

G2. This research located no supporting study for the following (they were outside the main search axis, so absence of evidence is all that can be claimed): the 20/40-second "traffic light", "leave while it is going well and keep half a story", and the "early on keep your insecurities to yourself" rule as stated. Follow-up questions (D3) are the evidence-backed alternative for the talk-too-much problem. Disclosure research (D3) supports gradual, reciprocal sharing.

## 2. Recommended changes to SKILL.md

Each item: ID, one instruction an assistant can follow, then the finding(s) it rests on (finding IDs refer to section 1).

### KEEP

KEEP-1. Keep "Ground them with facts" and the two-column "what you know / what you are guessing" exercise, and write each column item as a concrete, specific event (who said what, when). Rests on C5 (concrete processing reduces rumination), E1 (anxious minds detect rejection cues at a low threshold).

KEEP-2. Keep "Redirect the digging" in full. Rests on A3 (looking at the beloved's pictures increases infatuation), E4 (surveillance goes with more longing and distress), E1 (vigilance to threat cues).

KEEP-3. Keep the LOCKED plan rule and the "is the anxiety asking?" check, since repeated re-planning works like reassurance seeking. Rests on E2 (reassurance seeking runs through attachment anxiety and links to depression).

KEEP-4. Keep the self-harm protocol and add the local crisis number when known (988 in the US, emergency services for immediate danger). Rests on F2, F3.

KEEP-5. Keep the physiological sigh, worded as "a few minutes of slow breathing with long exhales", and suggest practising it daily in the days before the date. Rests on G1 (tested as 5 minutes daily for a month).

KEEP-6. Keep the internet-advice filter and add one more "toss" category: tactics that manufacture doubt or coldness. Rests on D1, D2, D3.

### CHANGE

CHANGE-1. Change the opening premise ("their heart runs the show") to: love feelings respond to where the user puts attention and how they think, so small daily choices shape them. Rests on A1, A3, A6 (love is regulable; people underestimate this).

CHANGE-2. Change "Split facts from fears" so its target is the anxiety while the love stays intact: for a spiral, offer distraction or an absorbing activity first; offer negative reappraisal of the person only to users who say they want to fall out of love (unavailable, unreciprocated, or harmful person), and warn that it lowers mood in the moment. Rests on A2, A3 (distraction lifts mood and leaves love unchanged; negative reappraisal lowers love and lowers mood).

CHANGE-3. Change "Suggest a professional only if the intensity eats sleep, food or work for days" to the concrete markers in ADD-4, raised kindly and briefly whenever a marker appears. Rests on F3, B4, B5 (the documented markers are neglect, intrusive distress, duration and recurrence; no validated day count exists).

CHANGE-4. Change the sleep rule: treat shorter sleep with good mood in the first weeks as common, and act when the user reports poor sleep quality, daytime exhaustion, or work slipping. Rests on B9 (early love: fewer hours, better subjective quality), A4 (task cost of mind-wandering).

CHANGE-5. Change the reminder example "Early on, keep your insecurities to yourself" to "Share yourself at the pace the other person shares, matching their depth and letting it grow both ways." Rests on D3 (Collins & Miller meta-analysis: disclosure and liking feed each other).

CHANGE-6. Change the "traffic light" tool to "after you speak, ask one follow-up question about what they just said." Rests on D3 (Huang et al.: follow-up questions raise liking and second-date agreement); G2 (no source located for the 20/40-second rule).

CHANGE-7. Change "Leave while it is going well" and "give it room" so they serve the user's own calm and pacing, and help the user reply warmly whenever they are ready to reply. Rests on D1 (weak, failed replication), D2 (hard-to-get backfires when read as disinterest), D3 (responsiveness builds attraction).

CHANGE-8. Change "this is the old wound talking" into a gentle question ("could this be the old story replaying?") paired with one present-tense fact about the new person. Rests on E1 (anxious hyperactivation reactivates past painful memories even during good moments); the coach has no basis to diagnose the source.

### ADD

ADD-1. Add a "normal or sticky" check in section 1: treat frequent, happy thoughts about the person as the normal state of early love, and work only on thoughts the user describes as intrusive, painful, or costly. Rests on A4 (67% of the time, mostly enjoyed), B5, B6 (intrusive thoughts tied to sadness and loneliness mark the impairing form).

ADD-2. When the user wants the thoughts gone, offer a short daily "person time" slot plus an if-then plan ("if the thought comes during work, I jot it down and bring it to 21:00"), followed by an absorbing task. Rests on C1, C2, C3 (suppression rebounds and raises arousal, more so with anxious attachment), C4 (the if-then version had the best single result; plain postponement often showed no effect).

ADD-3. Add a boundary rule, applied every time it comes up: when the user plans or reports repeated messages after no reply, checking where the person is, turning up uninvited, asking the person's friends for information, or continuing after a clear no, the coach names it as crossing the other person's boundary, stops helping with that plan, and helps the user step back. Rests on F1 (ORI definition and behaviours), F2 (love is associated with stalking).

ADD-4. Add concrete referral markers and raise professional help when any appears: work, study or close relationships being neglected; intrusive thoughts with sadness, loneliness or hopelessness most days; months of longing without reciprocation or the same pattern repeating across people; checking or rituals the user cannot stop; signs of anxiety or depression; any risk to self or the other person (crisis line at once); frame it around harm and well-being and suggest a therapist experienced with OCD-type or attachment problems. Rests on F2 (Earp: harm and well-being as the criterion), F3, B4, B5, B7, B8.

ADD-5. Add an anxious-attachment routine for a slow reply: name the alarm as a very sensitive rejection detector, list what the person has actually done that shows interest, agree one action and a time to check again, and invite the user to recall someone who reliably supports them. Rests on E1 (lowered threshold for unavailability cues), E3 (security reminders improve affect, more so in anxious people), C5.

ADD-6. Add an explanation for silence: when the user feels most intense while waiting, explain that not knowing makes the mind replay the person and this feels like stronger attraction, which tells you about the waiting. Rests on D1 (more thoughts in the uncertain condition; weak evidence), B1.

ADD-7. When the coach suggests writing, make it one short, concrete, time-limited entry about what happened and what the user will do next, especially for users who keep searching for meaning. Rests on C8 (small average effect; worse outcomes for meaning-searchers), C5.

ADD-8. Add activity as a first-line mood tool (a walk, a workout, time with people), especially when the user feels lonely or sad. Rests on A2 (distraction improves mood), B5 (intrusive fantasy peaks in sadness and loneliness), C7 (exercise reduces rumination, preliminary).

### REMOVE

REMOVE-1. Remove any implication that thinking about the person a lot is itself a problem (the opening line "their head is somewhere else" can stay as humour). Rests on finding A4.

REMOVE-2. Remove "once, briefly" for anything involving the other person's safety or boundaries; those are said every time they come up. Rests on F1, F2.

REMOVE-3. Remove the "for days" threshold wording, replaced by ADD-4. Rests on F3.

## 3. Gaps and unverified items

- Langeslag & Sanchez N=24 from secondary sources only.
- Langeslag & Philippi sample size not available.
- Spitzberg & Cupach 2007 prevalence figures not verified.
- Behavioral activation for rumination: no primary source verified.
